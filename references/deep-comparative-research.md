# Deep reference study — lessons that survive beyond award aesthetics

**Research round:** 2 Nov 2026. No designs are copied or selected. *Maker statements and published figures are sourced; interpretative transfers to Darb are labelled as proposed.* Award is evidence of recognition, **not** a substitute for fit, accessibility or impact.

## 1 — Getty x Resn / Tracing Art, 2025
Primary production interview: https://www.commarts.com/project/38649/tracing-art

**Material problem:** a very large, ambiguous provenance index whose connections are hard for non-experts to grasp. **Editorial move:** make *records → stories → linked subjects*, while respecting historical uncertainty. **Reported production scale:** 400 images, a 16-person Getty+Resn team and 13 weeks concept-to-launch. **Tech explicitly disclosed:** GSAP ScrollTrigger, Lenis and three.js for portions of the work, with sprite-based particles for an art sphere.

**Critical reading for Darb:** The “wow” is not the sphere. It's a convincing information bridge from historical documents to discoverable human contexts. If Darb's collection contains inconsistent years, artist names, programme records, loss and rebuilding stories, the first design task is to establish **relational editorial truth**, then give those links a visual rhythm.

**Experiment proposal:** build paper storyboard linking 2023 Something Else exhibition → p.17 image #62 → a named credited artist → specific place/date → another project. If artist credit is missing, show it as a missing record internally instead of fabricating a link. No 3D required.

**Wrong lesson:** Full-page scroll choreography is mandatory for an acclaimed archive. It's not; Tracing Art had a large team and highly structured materials. Darb's source photos are mixed resolution and require curation first.

## 2 — National Gallery x Fabrique x Q42 / Imaginarium, 2025–26
Maker: https://www.q42.nl/en/work/imaginarium-national-gallery
Award: https://winners.webbyawards.com/2026/websites-and-mobile-sites/general-desktop-mobile-sites/cultural-institutions/380455/national-gallery-imaginarium

**Actual goal:** digital ‘slow looking’ — viewers spend more time looking at paintings through curator-guided questions. **Maker-reported stack:** Three.js, React, R3F, Micrio, Sanity, Firebase. A dreamlike room and AI motion are described, but **these are solutions to the National Gallery brief**, not general best practices.

**Critical reading for Darb:** Consider the *attention* model instead of the floating-painting scene. One high-quality Darb photograph (#41 cyanotype or #21 white textile installation) could be experienced slowly with a true curator annotation, an artist's explanation and a readable caption. The piece should not be AI-animated or treated as a texture without artist approval.

**Experiment:** write three non-leading viewing prompts based on a verified artwork/creator. Allow a quiet view, explicit details and text fallback. Ask artist to validate the interpretation.

## 3 — Detroit Institute of Arts x Purple Rock Scissors / Digital museum redesign
Maker: https://www.purplerockscissors.com/work/detroit-institute-of-arts

**Problem:** discovery of events vs exhibitions, and navigation of large image collections. **Design move:** separate calendar and exhibition content, improve visitor planning and art exploration while using actual museum architectural motifs and subtle motion. **Studio's reported outcomes** (not independently audited): +49% time exploring collection, +58% events/calendar traffic and +50% visit-planning traffic. DIA's collection contains 60k+ works, not comparable in scale to Darb's initial 77 objects.

**Critical reading for Darb:** the arts site needs utility: avoid a gorgeous home that forces a local visitor through a manifesto when looking for the next film night. Website goals and content types can be physically separated and then visually reunited.

**Experiment:** a paper navigation test: find an event in 2 steps; find an exhibition archive record in 2 steps; a verified venue page cannot show a dead or historical location as open now.

## 4 — Clyfford Still Museum x Cooper / Collection archive (2025)
Maker (designer case): https://cooper.digital/archive.html

**Process evidence:** artist/designer describes a **three-day staff workshop** exploring information architecture, wireframe prototypes and art direction, using actual CMS data. Multiple entry modes were explored: spotlight articles, visual maps, immersive timelines, shared user collections.

**Critical reading for Darb:** the staff's editorial data must drive the archive language. We shouldn't build a 3D timeline just because another museum did. Our high-uncertainty requirements — current venue, image credits, permissions, whether artist pages can be kept up to date — demand a short staff workflow audit.

**Experiment:** fill one true programme template with 5 credited assets, AR/EN copy and registration links; see what fields are absent and how to handle them honestly.

## 5 — Rijksmuseum x Q42/Fabrique (2020 redesign / ED Gold 2021)
Maker https://www.q42.nl/en/work/rijks-website1
Award https://awards.europeandesign.org/winner/248792

**Problem:** millions of source objects exist, but a collection is not meaningful as an undirected table. **Decision:** huge images combined with a “Stories” editorial layer and audience-specific discovery. **Critical transfer:** if the selected artwork deserves attention, the interface should step away; but when it is a factual photograph of a workshop, captions and consent are equally important.

**Experiment:** compare an item-first approach (artwork → author → exhibition) against a story-first approach (exhibition → scene → item) for the same Darb images.

## 6 — No Problem: The People / Darb 1718 related creative work (Cairo 2026)
Primary project statement by curator Power Ekroth: https://www.powerekroth.net/noproblemthepeople

**Why it belongs in the website research:** it is not a digital design reference. It is **source material for Darb's own ethical and artistic language**. The curator centres labour that often goes unseen — technicians, caretakers, administrators, neighbours and artists. Nourhan Elmeligy is publicly acknowledged as a contributor, without specifying an exact professional title.

**Critical transfer:** instead of heroizing one person or making 2024 the only dramatic narrative, the website can make **credit a navigation system** and collaboration visible. This is a proposal requiring the people involved to be asked for permission.

## Evidence vs interpretations
- **Published:** specific awards, studio claims, tech disclosures, signed exhibition statements.
- **Interpretation:** why a solution could support Darb, and what hypotheses should be tested.
- **Unknown:** each external site's audited performance, precise CMS costs, Darb's current editorial capacity, artist permissions, venue/location and exact event dependencies.
- **Not allowed:** fabricate quantitative results for Darb, claim any stage is live, or call a reference award-winning without award-body evidence.

## Cross-reference: which lesson matters MOST now?
| Core concern | Best source of principle | Before motion/visuals, test |
|---|---|---|
| Incomplete historic archive | Getty Tracing Art | Can every relational link be sourced? |
| Deep image engagement | Imaginarium | Can an artwork's credited meaning justify attention? |
| Utility amid creativity | Detroit Institute of Arts | Are What’s On, visit and applications immediate? |
| Staff and source truth | Clyfford Still Museum | Can staff publish and correct an event? |
| Works as stories | Rijksmuseum | Are art images adequately resolved and captioned? |
| Unique Darb meaning | No Problem: The People | Are unseen makers genuinely credited? |

## Proposed research rubric (subjective, not award judging)
The strongest website will combine: **Darb's distinctive lived truth** + **excellent editorial/story architecture** + **understandable visitor journeys** + **careful art presentation** + **bilingual modern visual authorship**, and only then **optional expressive technology**.
