## High-confidence breakthrough: a specific Jusoor film has an independent author text

### Film: Braids of Identity / جدائل الهوية
**Evidence A — Darb's own supplied Overview 2026 PDF p.9:** lists *Braids of Identity*, filmmaker Hafsa Baraei / حفصة برعي, within a Sudanese short-film showcase under the Jusoor programme. This establishes a **film → Darb programme** relationship, not an exact screening time or audience Q&A on that specific film.

**Evidence B — filmmaker-attributed museum publication:** [SSLH Museum: جدائل الهوية](https://www.sslh.info/ar/posts/braids-of-identity), bylined **حفصة برعي**, published **18 June 2025** in the page's metadata. It describes the film as documenting the Sudanese traditional hairdressing/braiding craft *al-mashat* (حرفة المشاط), its history, social/economic role and threats to its continuity. The text connects documentation to care for living heritage. This page provides **artist/author context we did not possess** in the initial Darb PDF alone.

**Image credit caution:** the SSLH page states **cover photograph © أماني بشير (Amani Bashir)**. This is the cover **of that museum article**; it does NOT automatically establish that Amani Bashir photographed the film, created its poster, or owns Darb's PDF p.9 image. The SSLH page's image gallery content is not a guaranteed direct film still/clip. Do not rehost the image without permission.

**Supported factual graph:**
- FILM “جدائل الهوية” → FILMMAKER “حفصة برعي” (Darb p.9 + SSLH publication).
- FILM → PROGRAMME “Jusoor” (Darb p.9).
- FILM → SUBJECT “حرفة المشاط السوداني” (filmmaker-attributed SSLH museum text).
- MUSEUM PUBLICATION → AUTHOR “حفصة برعي”; publication cover photo credited “أماني بشير”.

**Crucial distinctions:**
- This DOES NOT prove a film license, trailer, still, date of a Darb screening, exact director attendance, English approved title romanization, or the publication's cover image rights for the Darb site.
- SSLH Museum publication is an independent source, not Darb's official artist/curator approval; confirm with filmmaker/team before authored interactive interpretation.
- Do not assume the Sudanese craft is directly related to Fustat craftspeople; cross-programme resemblance is only an editorial *comparison*.

**Why it materially improves the creative direction:** We can now propose a *real* exploration of documented craft through filmmaker research, film, the credited filmmaker and the Jusoor programme — **provided** visual/audio media and authored interpretation arrive. The interaction question becomes: “How can digital film viewing foreground a craft's social and economic dimension?” Not “which background or gallery carousel should we use?”
